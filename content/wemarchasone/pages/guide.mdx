## What We March as One is

The store short description is one sentence:

> Start with a few shapes and build an army tens of thousands strong. Paint your troops onto the battlefield and send them to war against enormous hordes in this incremental auto battler where every unit is individually simulated.

The longer text opens with the same idea in fewer words: We March as One is an incremental-style auto battler about building a massive army of shapes.

Steam’s genre field is Indie, Simulation, and Strategy. The player category is Single-player. Family Sharing is also listed. There is no co-op or multiplayer category in the 12 Nov 2026 payload. The store page showed no user reviews, and the release block said the game is not yet available.

User tags returned by the store browse API that day, in weight order, were Incremental, Simulation, Strategy, Auto Battler, War, Tower Defense, Economy, Sandbox, Wargame, Tactical RPG, Tactical, RTS, Turn-Based Tactics, RPG, Minimalist, Physics, Abstract, Strategy RPG, Singleplayer, and Roguelike. Those tags are not the genre field. They can change, and a tag is not a system the about text described. The about text’s own phrase is incremental auto battler. It does not say the battles are turn-based.

## The loop the store describes

Nothing in the sources checked is a build order. The safe sequence is the one the store headings repeat.

**Paint your army.** You paint troops directly onto the battlefield however you like, and you position them to counter the enemy force. That is the whole of the published placement rule. See [Units](/units).

**Upgrade your units.** You earn gold from defeated enemies, whether you win or lose the battle. You recruit more troops, unlock new unit types from a branching skill tree, and return with a stronger army. Fallen troops return for the next battle, so you keep the army you have built. See [Progression](/progression).

**Massive battles.** Every soldier and projectile is individually simulated. Isometric, top-down, or fly-through modes are the three ways the store says you can view the action.

**Sandbox mode.** The store lists a level editor, and it says you can experiment with unusual army compositions across different battlefield layouts.
